# Solitude, twice: Montaigne and Thoreau

## The two sources

- Montaigne, [Of Solitude](../essayists/montaigne/of-solitude.md) (Cotton's translation).
- Thoreau, [Solitude](../walden/thoreau/solitude.md), from *Walden*.

## What the sources say

Montaigne argues that leaving the world is useless unless one also leaves the habits one carries: we must "disentangle ourselves from those violent importunities that engage us elsewhere and separate us from ourselves." Thoreau, alone at the pond, insists he is never lonely: "I have a great deal of company in my house; especially in the morning, when nobody calls."

## Interpretation

Montaigne's solitude is a discipline; Thoreau's is a discovery. One prepares for it, the other finds he was already there. Both put the difficulty inside the person, not in the woods or the tower.
