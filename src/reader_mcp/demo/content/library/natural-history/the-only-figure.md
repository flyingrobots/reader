# The only figure in the Origin

*On the Origin of Species* (1859) has exactly one illustration: a fold-out diagram of branching lineages in the fourth chapter.

![Darwin's diagram of divergence, from On the Origin of Species (1859). Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.](darwin-diagram.png)

*Figure. Letters A–L along the bottom are species of one genus; each horizontal line is a thousand generations or more. Most lines stop. A few branch and keep going.*

## What the diagram claims

Darwin uses it to argue two things at once: that varieties diverge as they are selected, and that most lineages go extinct. The dotted fan lines are possible variations; only some survive to the next row.

## Why it matters for reading the chapters

The [Struggle for Existence](struggle-for-existence.md) supplies the pressure; the diagram shows its shape over time; the [Recapitulation](recapitulation.md) closes with the same image in words: "endless forms most beautiful and most wonderful."
